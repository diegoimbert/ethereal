# Trademark and brand policy

The name **Ethereal**, the Ethereal logo and the icons in [`assets/brand/`](assets/brand)
(together, "the Marks") identify the official project maintained by Diego Imbert. The code is
free software under the AGPL (see [LICENSING.md](LICENSING.md)), but the license covers the
code, not the Marks: it grants no trademark rights (AGPL-3.0 section 7(e)). This policy says
how you can use them, so that people can tell the official Ethereal from other builds.

## Fine without asking

- Referring to the project by name: in articles, reviews, tutorials, videos, talks, courses
  and social posts, including with the logo to illustrate them.
- Redistributing the **unmodified** official builds under their name.
- Saying that something works with Ethereal, in a way that is accurate and does not suggest
  it is official or endorsed: "presets for Ethereal", "a plugin tested with Ethereal".
- Building and running modified versions for yourself or inside your organization.
- Community groups and events about Ethereal, provided they make clear they are unofficial.

## Needs permission

- Distributing or hosting a **modified** version (a fork, a patched build, a hosted instance)
  under the name or with the logo. Rename it and replace the icons; you may say it is "based
  on Ethereal".
- Using the Marks in the name of a product, a service, a company, a domain name or an account
  handle, or on merchandise.
- Anything that suggests the project or its maintainer sponsors, endorses or supports you.
- Using a modified version of the logo, or a name or logo confusingly similar to the Marks.

## Using the logo

Use the files in [`assets/brand/`](assets/brand) as they are: don't change their shape,
colors or proportions, and keep some space around them.

## Asking

Ask [@diegoimbert](https://github.com/diegoimbert) on GitHub. If you are unsure whether your
use is fine, ask: the answer is usually yes for good-faith, clearly unofficial uses.

This policy may change; the version on `main` applies.
